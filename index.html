03 / RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT
Undergraduate research 2026

NEUROFAST: Reducing Calibration Time in Motor Imagery BCIs through Early Learning Prediction

Brain-computer interface systems typically require calibration before users can effectively perform motor imagery tasks. However, individuals differ in how quickly they learn to control these systems. This research investigates whether learning speed can be predicted using only the first 40% of calibration trials.

The proposed approach combines an EEGNet-based feature encoder with a four-head self-attention aggregation mechanism to classify users into good and poor learners. The work explores the potential of early prediction to support more adaptive BCI training and reduce unnecessary calibration effort.

61.3% Balanced accuracy
76 Subjects evaluated using leave-one-subject-out validation
40% Initial calibration data used
+11.3 Percentage points over majority-class baseline

Results are from the evaluated PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery dataset. The findings are preliminary and do not establish clinical effectiveness.
